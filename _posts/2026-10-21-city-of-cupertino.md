Cupertino is a city in Santa Clara County, California — and it's one of those places most people only know through a single company. That's a shame. There's more to it than the Apple Park, and I wanted to take a few notes on what makes it worth a closer look.

## The Apple Factor

It's impossible to write about Cupertino without the elephant in the valley: Apple. The campus — Apple Park — is an architectural landmark in its own right, surrounded by unusually large buffers of lawns, fountains, and security. You can see it from the public sidewalks and the nearby parks, though the grounds themselves are Apple's and aren't open to the public. Still, the building is pleasing to look at from almost every angle, and it's a reminder of how much of the city's identity is tied to the company.

## A City of Contrasts

What surprises people is how residential Cupertino feels. With roughly 60,000 residents and one of the highest median household incomes in the country, it's a city of large, well-kept homes and leafy streets. There's a strong Chinese-American culture here, visible in the many restaurants, markets, and schools, and the downtown core is anchored by the library and the community center, with a handful of cafés and restaurants rather than a sprawling downtown.

## A Walk Through the Neighborhoods

There's also the separate neighborhood of North Cupertino, with its own shops and a much more laid-back rhythm. And if you get the chance, the foothills of the South Bay are beautiful on a cool morning. Cupertino itself is compact — the main drag, Lawrence Expressway, cuts across the city in minutes — which makes it easy to spend an afternoon without feeling rushed.

## Notes for Next Time

If you visit, skip the corporate tours and just wander. The neighborhood is fine for a casual stroll, and a coffee at one of the local cafés is a good way to see how the city actually lives. It's not a postcard town in the usual sense, but it's a real one, and that's what makes it interesting.
